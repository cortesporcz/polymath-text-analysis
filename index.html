4) Tokenize Texts

Tokenization breaks text into smaller units called ‘tokens’. Here, each token is a word -
For example: ‘Nature is a living whole’ becomes:
nature
is
a
living
whole

Breaking the text apart allows R to count and compare individual words. The words(language) are being turned into data that can be counted.

words <- texts %>%
  unnest_tokens(word, text)

5) Remove Stop Words

Words such as “the”, “and”, “of”, “to” and “in” occur frequently but often add little value for analysis. For this analysis, we remove common stop words so other vocabulary becomes easier to see.

This is a researcher decision based on the choice of the texts being studied and the researcher. Removing stop words is a methodological choice. Some common words may matter, depending on the context.

data("stop_words")

words_clean <- words %>%
  anti_join(stop_words, by = "word")

6) Normalize variations of certain words

words_clean <- words_clean %>%
  mutate(
    word = case_when(
      word %in% c(
        "colour",
        "colours",
        "coloured",
        "color",
        "colors",
        "colored"
      ) ~ "colour",
      TRUE ~ word
    )
  )

R doesn’t understand that colour, colours, coloured, and the American spelling ‘color’ are related concepts. To R, they’re simply different strings of characters. For my research question, I’m more interested in references to the concept of colour or color, rather than differences between these grammatical forms or spellings. So, I’m telling R to treat these variants as one term: colour. This is called ‘normalization’.

words_clean %>%

9) Take patterns and go back to the text

Text analysis can identify patterns, however, a frequency count doesn’t discuss what words mean in context.

I created questions that I think I would ask or discuss with the texts to continue the inquiry:

  1. Are there shared vocabularies among these authors?
  2. Which authors appear more concerned with nature?
  3. Which authors appear more concerned with society or history?
  4. What are the limitations of word-frequency analysis?
  5. How might translations affect patterns?
